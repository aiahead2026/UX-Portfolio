Expense Tracker

A lightweight household expense app, designed, built, and used every day.

Project Overview

Expense Tracker is a progressive web app I designed and built to track household spending. It installs on a phone like a native app and keeps every purchase, category, and monthly total in one place. Its tagline sums up the intent: small steps, clear totals.

I've been using it daily for the past couple of months, and it keeps growing based on what my household actually needs.

The challenge

Most budgeting apps are either too heavy for quick, everyday use or too generic to match how a household actually shops. I wanted something fast enough to log an expense in seconds, simple enough that anyone at home could use it, and clear enough to show where the money goes each month without extra effort.

My role

I'm the designer and developer on this project. That included:

  • Defining the features from everyday use rather than assumptions
  • Designing a dark, card-based interface with pill-style navigation that works one-handed on a phone
  • Building it as a single-file app in plain HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with Supabase for data and Vercel for hosting
  • Writing a clear spec for each new feature before building it, so additions never disturb what already works

The Solution

  • Dashboard: an interactive category chart with a clickable legend, plus a breakdown by store and category.
  • Quick entry: a short form with month selection, alphabetized categories, and a confirmation step. Past months lock automatically to keep records clean.
  • All Expenses: a full list with inline edit and delete.
  • Category management: add, rename, or remove categories, with changes carried through past expenses.
  • Installable and offline-ready: it lives on the home screen like a native app.

New feature: Shopping list

The most recent addition grew directly out of daily use. Shopping and spending happen together, so the app now includes a shared shopping list:

  • Grocery categories always visible, each with its own quick-add button
  • Items check off in place with a strikethrough, so the list stays readable mid-shop
  • A Done shopping button that appears once everything is checked
  • A history view to look back at past trips

Outcomes & What's next

After a couple of months of daily use, the app has replaced guesswork with clear monthly totals, and the shopping list has become part of the household routine. More features are on the way, including:

  • A shared login for the household
  • Voice input for logging expenses hands-free
  • Backdated entry for recent months
  • Budgets per category, month-over-month trends, and savings goals

Reflection

Designing for my own household made feedback immediate: if something was awkward, I felt it the next day. The discipline of adding features without disturbing the existing design, and specifying each one before building it, kept a growing app simple to use.
