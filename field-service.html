Pega Field Service

My Journey as a Lead UX Designer on Pega Field Service

Project Duration and My InvolvementCrafting Key User ExperiencesImpact and Reflections

Over several years, I had the privilege of serving as a key UX designer on the Pega Field Service project—a long-term initiative that evolved into a comprehensive, end-to-end field service management solution. Built on the Pega Platform and layered atop Pega Customer Service, this application transformed how utilities, telecommunications, and other service-heavy industries handle everything from initial customer requests to on-site resolutions. My focus was crafting intuitive, role-specific user experiences that empowered diverse users—customer service reps, dispatchers, field technicians, and administrators—while ensuring seamless integration across the ecosystem.

Project Duration and My Involvement

I contributed to this project across multiple years and releases, from earlier Pega versions through ongoing enhancements in the Infinity era (up to '24.x and beyond). It was an iterative process, involving user research, prototyping, usability testing, and refinements based on real-world feedback. This multi-year commitment allowed us to mature the solution from foundational components into a polished, AI-enhanced product.

Crafting Key User Experiences

As the UX designer, I designed interconnected portals and apps that form the heart of Pega Field Service. The solution is composed of several specialized experiences, each tailored to its user's needs:

  • Customer Service Application: I designed this for CSRs to handle interactions efficiently, log service requests, and kick off field jobs—all within the familiar Pega Customer Service framework for a smooth, contextual flow.
  • Dispatcher Portal: One of my favorite challenges; I crafted this intuitive workbench with timeline views, interactive maps, and real-time scheduling tools. Dispatchers can visualize field workers, optimize routes, and make quick adjustments, reducing complexity in high-pressure environments.
  • Mobile Application for Field Technicians: I focused heavily on mobility here, creating a native-like experience that supports offline mode, job updates, digital signatures, barcode scanning, and photo capture. The goal was to make technicians' days easier and more productive, even in remote or low-connectivity areas.
  • Admin Portal: I shaped this for seamless setup of customers, locations, skills, and configurations, ensuring administrators have straightforward tools to maintain the system's backbone.

These experiences are unified on the low-code Pega Platform, allowing for customization, dynamic case management, and features like predictive scheduling—all while maintaining a consistent design language.

Impact and Reflections

Through user-centered design, we delivered a solution that minimizes downtime, boosts technician efficiency, elevates customer satisfaction, and drives cost savings. It was incredibly rewarding to see how these crafted experiences centralized fragmented processes into one cohesive platform.
