FlipItOrNot

Turning my own fix-and-flip deal analysis into a guided web app. Work in progress.

Project OverviewThe challengeMy roleThe SolutionWhere it standsReflection

Project Overview

FlipItOrNot is a web app I'm designing and building to answer one question for real estate investors: should I flip this property or walk away? It started as my own process for evaluating fix-and-flip deals in the Raleigh area, a mix of spreadsheets, county records, and notes, and is becoming a single guided flow that ends in a clear verdict.

The project is in active development. The core analysis flow works end to end, and I'm continuing to refine it release by release.

I built FlipItOrNot in close collaboration with AI. I set the product direction, designed the experience, and made every call on scope and trade-offs. AI worked alongside me as a pair programmer and reviewer, helping me write and debug code, pressure-test the plan, and move from idea to working software far faster than I could alone. It's a hands-on example of how I see design evolving: designers who can shape a product and ship it.

The challenge

Evaluating a flip means pulling together many moving parts: comparable sales, rehab scope, holding costs, and financing, often under a short due-diligence window. Doing it by hand is slow, and it's easy to miss a cost or lean on the wrong comps. The goal was a tool that structures that thinking, so every deal is judged the same way and the reasoning behind each decision stays visible.

My role

I'm the designer, product owner, and developer on this project. That meant:

  • Mapping my own analysis process into a step-by-step flow
  • Designing the interface, visual system, and component patterns
  • Building the app with Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and Supabase, deployed on Vercel
  • Importing and cleaning about 40,000 Wake County single-family sales records to power comp selection
  • Learning to code along the way, with each phase reviewed and tested before moving to the next
  • Partnering with AI as a pair programmer and reviewer throughout the build

The Solution

The app guides each deal through six steps, each one building on the last:

  • Dashboard: every deal at a glance, with its verdict, the reason behind it, and how many comps support it.
  • Deal Detail: the property basics that anchor the analysis.
  • Comp Selection: comparable sales scored by proximity, so the strongest matches rise to the top and can be picked or dropped.
  • Rehab and Holding Costs: structured inputs for scope of work, plus the carrying costs that quietly erode a margin.
  • Summary & Review: everything on one screen before committing to a decision.
  • Analysis & Verdict: a clear call, with the numbers and reasoning that support it.

Where it stands

The full analysis flow, from Dashboard to Verdict, works end to end on real county data. Next on the roadmap:

  • Polishing how comps are presented in the summary
  • PDF export to share an analysis with partners and lenders
  • Deal history and a pipeline view to track opportunities over time
  • A security review of data access rules

Reflection

Building this tool changed how I think about my own design work. Being the user, the designer, and the developer at once made every trade-off concrete: a confusing step slowed me down the next time I analyzed a deal. It's also a reminder that the best way to structure a complex decision is to make the reasoning visible, not just the result.
